How the mint works

1

Mint price. Each intern costs $20 in ETH plus 999 $STONKBROKER during the first 24 hours. The ETH leg is priced by the Chainlink ETH/USD feed at mint time and stays a flat $20 for the life of the mint: 25% of it funds the Intern Clock In engine and the rest goes to the treasury. The $STONKBROKER leg goes to the treasury and is the only part of the price that moves (step 5).

2

Every intern is born in its broker's wallet. The moment the mint opens, a permissionless sweep mints all 8,888 interns into the token bound wallet of their parent broker as dormant. The full supply exists onchain and shows on marketplaces from day one, full art included. A dormant intern cannot be moved, activated, named, traded on the Intern Exchange, or used as collateral.

3

Only the parent can mint it out. To release a broker's interns you must own that StonkBroker and it must be activated. Paying the mint fee moves the intern out of the broker wallet into your wallet, deploys the intern's own token bound wallet, and makes it live. Release the V1 intern, the V2 intern, or both in a single transaction.

4

24 hour opening window. For the first 24 hours after the mint opens the $STONKBROKER leg sits at its opening price of 999 $STONKBROKER. This is the cheapest the mint will ever be.

5

The $STONKBROKER leg compounds every day after the window. For the first 10 days after the window closes it rises 10% per day: 1,098 on day 1, 1,208 on day 2, 1,329 on day 3, reaching 2,591 on day 10. For the next 365 days it rises 1% per day, landing at about 97,902 $STONKBROKER on day 375, and holds there. Each step compounds on the previous day's price. The ETH leg is a flat $20 the whole time. The contract owner can only lower the opening price or either daily step, never raise them.

Job titles

The broker owner gives each intern a slice of that broker's Clock In revenue. The title stored in the intern's metadata is rank times desk. The slice sets the rank: 0.25% Trainee · 0.5% Junior · 1% no prefix · 2% Senior · 2.5% Head. The parent broker's live activation tier sets the desk: Floor Trader · Floor Runner, Analyst · Research Intern, Portfolio Manager · Portfolio Intern, Managing Director · Executive Intern, Partner · Partner Track Intern. So a Floor Trader delegating 2.5% has a Head Floor Runner, a Partner delegating 0.5% has a Junior Partner Track Intern, and a broker that upgrades its own tier promotes both interns the same block. Titles only move up. A promotion is a small ETH fee, a demotion is not possible, and the title stays with the intern when it changes hands. If the parent broker is not activated the slice pauses and the title reads Applicant until the broker is activated again; a dormant intern that has not been minted out yet reads Applicant as well. The pay share sets this slice and the title, and nothing else: it does not change the intern's weight in the intern Clock In pot.

Intern Clock In

Interns run their own Clock In, the Intern Clock In 3.0 engine, separate from the Broker Clock In 3.0 pool (both addresses are in the contract table). It is live: the collection's 9.99% royalties, the 25% mint share and every desk the interns operate pay into it, and a round opens whenever 0.01 ETH has accrued. Each intern's weight is its own activation tier only. The ladder has five tiers paid in $STONKBROKER: 3,333 / 8,333 / 18,333 / 33,333 / 83,333, named Freshman, Sophomore, Junior, Senior, Alumnus at 1.00x / 1.25x / 1.60x / 2.00x / 3.33x and shown as the intern's Activation Tier trait. Two Freshmen earn the same whatever their pay share; an Alumnus earns 3.33x a Freshman; payout per round is weight divided by total weight times the pot. Interns elect from the same 14 token menu as brokers, default to $STONKBROKER when they have no election on file, and claim their own pay from My Interns; payouts land in the intern's own token bound wallet. The first 24 hours of Intern Clock In were built differently on purpose: the pot weighted each intern by pay share as well as tier, so raising an intern to Head (2.5%) was a 10x boost. That encouraged broker owners to put their interns on payroll. From the generation 2 engine the pot weighs the activation ladder only; the pay share keeps paying the parent broker slice.

How an intern gets paid, in full

Parent broker slice. The parent StonkBroker's own Clock In credits (broker Clock In 3.0). Every credit the parent broker earns splits share% to this intern's wallet. The broker owner sets the share: 0.25% floor (Trainee), 0.5% (Junior), 1%, 2% (Senior) or 2.5% (Head), at most 5% per broker across both interns, up only, and it follows the intern when it is sold. If the parent broker is inactive: If the parent broker is not activated (sold, or never activated) it earns no credits, so this slice pauses. Intern Exchange

An automated market for interns, run by the interns. Deposit $STONKBROKER, interns, or both into a pool, set a buy price, a sell price and a step, and the pool market makes on its own: every buy moves the price up one step, every sell moves it down. Pools are quoted in ETH or in $STONKBROKER. Browse it in an Anvil style vault view or a marketplace style grid.

Names marketplace for Robinhood Chain

The interns operate the onchain name service for all of Robinhood Chain. Anyone can register a name that resolves to a wallet, list it and trade it, and the interns benefit from the fees the marketplace earns. An intern can carry a name of its own that travels with it when it is sold. Fees and the ENS gateway details are published before launch.

V2 lending for brokers and interns

The interns operate the V2 lending desk for StonkBrokers and interns. Liquidity providers supply ETH or $STONKBROKER, borrowers post a StonkBroker or a live intern as collateral, and liquidations honor the StonkBroker royalty. The interns earn the fees the desk generates. Parameters are published before the desk opens.

Interns desks (Anvil AMM)

The desk is the Anvil 2 way functionality for NFTs: user run order books with a price ladder. Anyone opens a desk for any ERC-721 on Robinhood Chain (quoted in ETH, STONKBROKER, USDG or one of the listed stock tokens) or on Ethereum mainnet (ETH, WBTC, USDT, USDC, APE, XAUt), and loads NFTs (an ask desk), quote (a bid desk) or both (an Anvil 2 way desk). Traders buy from the cheapest ask and sell into the highest bid; the floor sorts every listing by USD. Live at /marketplace?tab=desks with a Robinhood / Ethereum switch; the Ethereum floor pins the top collections (BAYC, MAYC, Wrapped CryptoPunks, Pudgy Penguins, Azuki, Doodles, Milady, Moonbirds, CloneX, Lil Pudgys) into the picker.

How a desk prices

  • Ladder. Each NFT moves the price once. Linear: the ask steps up by a fixed amount after every sale and the bid steps down after every buy. Exponential: the same with a percent of the current price (a 2% step makes each rung 1.02x the last). A batch walks the rungs one by one: buying five at once pays one step above spot for the first and five steps above for the fifth, the same total as five single buys back to back, and selling five into a bid walks the bid rungs down the same way.
  • Fee. 9.99% of the curve value in the quote token on every trade: 66% back into the desk that filled it (the desk compounds), 25% to the intern Clock In engine, 9% to the treasury. Buyers see the fee included price; sellers see the net they receive. A two way desk may add its own spread of up to 20%.
  • Floors. StonkBrokers asks can never go under 733,332 STONKBROKER (one seat plus one activation) and are quoted in STONKBROKER until the protocol pins a floor for another quote. Bids are never floor gated. A two way desk that buys a broker back keeps its next ask at the floor.
  • Safety. The exchange is the only contract that can move a desk's NFTs or quote for a trade; only the desk owner can load, withdraw or reprice. Every buy carries a price limit and every sale a minimum, so a repriced desk reverts instead of overcharging. Fee sinks that cannot receive park their leg instead of blocking the trade; anyone can flush it later. Owner powers are limited to lowering the fee, steering the split within the 25% intern floor, delaying sink changes by 24 hours, pausing trading (desk withdrawals never pause) and blocking a collection.

Selling an intern or a broker through a desk

Moving an activated broker or intern into a desk, or selling it into a bid, clears its activation on the way out. The buyer re activates. Interns only trade through desks once the Stonk Interns owner has registered the exchange on the intern transfer validator (`setPoolRegistry`); brokers and every collection without a transfer validator trade immediately.

Safety Deposit Box: liquidity locker

Lock LP on Robinhood Chain to prove liquidity commitments while keeping swap-fee income. Fee modes (chosen at lock time, immutable)

0.5% upfront

Protocol takes 0.5% of the locked liquidity at lock time. Nothing more on withdraw or fee-collect.

20% of swap fees

Protocol takes 20% of every swap-fee collect (80/20 split). Locked principal is never touched. Offered for Special Partners.

A legacy third mode (1% on withdraw) still functions for locks that chose it but is no longer offered for new locks.

Lock styles and unlock windows

Each lock sets a start and finish timestamp: nothing is withdrawable before start, liquidity vests linearly between the two, and after finish the position is fully withdrawable and releasable. The UI offers three styles built on that window:

Hard lock

Start is set to the unlock date with a 10-minute vest tail after it, 0% withdrawable until the date, then everything. The default for new locks.

Linear vesting

Start is now (or a chosen delay) and finish is the end date, liquidity unlocks continuously across the window, withdrawable as it vests.

Permanent

Window pinned at the top of the uint64 range, vesting never begins and release is impossible for anyone, forever. Swap fees stay claimable.

Lock cards badge every deposit as HARD LOCK, LINEAR VESTING, or PERMANENT so the semantics are readable at a glance. The UI stamps windows a few minutes ahead at submit so transactions can't revert with a past start time; this only ever delays a window, never shortens it.

Where protocol fees go

All locker protocol fees route to the Safety Deposit Clock In contract, an ownerless, pull-based router with a 90% community / 10% protocol split. ETH and WETH flow into the Stock Booster reward pot; other tokens open claim rounds that activated brokers pull into their token-bound wallets under the rewards program, each broker claiming its own tier-weighted allocation.

Safety properties

  • No admin key can seize, withdraw, or release locked liquidity, the owner can only rotate the fee recipient for future fees.
  • Locked principal is mathematically unwithdrawable before the unlock window opens; permanent locks can never be withdrawn by anyone.
  • Only the current holder of the lock's ownership NFT can collect swap fees, withdraw vested liquidity, or release the position.
  • Lock windows are fixed inside the signed transaction, no third party can shorten them after the fact.

Opening Bell buybacks: VRNG-triggered market buys

Every bonding-curve trade pays the launcher fee, and a fixed slice of every fee streams into the Buyback Bar, an on-chain pot that charges as the floor trades. When the bar is full, the miner-backed VRNG (the same certified-print entropy that runs Broker Box) draws two things nobody, not traders, not miners, not the team, can predict or forge: when the bell becomes ring-able, and which live bonding-curve token gets bought.

1. The bar charges

A portion of every buy and sell on every bonding curve feeds the pot. More floor volume = faster charge. The bar level is public, everyone watches it climb.

2. The draw

VRNG picks the moment and the target token in one draw. Selection odds are weighted by each token's fee contribution, curves that feed the bar more carry proportionally better odds, but every live token has a shot on every draw.

3. Clock In

Once the bell goes live, anyone can Clock In to ring it. The entire bar market-buys the drawn token straight into its curve in a single sweep, and the ringer earns a tip for pulling the trigger.

On every other launchpad the fee is pure exit, value leaves the floor and never comes back. Here the house edge is pointed back at the tokens themselves: fees recycle into random, unfrontrunnable buybacks, so expected value flows back to the people actually trading. It is designed to be the highest-RTP meme-coin trading floor crypto has ever seen, the entropy path is byte-identical to Broker Box's audited draw (a pure function of a future certified print), so buyback timing can't be sniped, ground, or selectively aborted by anyone, including us.

How Stonk Certificates work

1. Struck fully onchain

Claiming a win (or buying at the counter) mints the next numbered deed into one shared collection. Artwork is drawn entirely by the smart contract, no servers, no image hosting. Traits are minimal by policy: Stock, Vaulted Amount, Branch.

2. Sealed vault inside

Every deed owns an onchain vault holding the actual stock tokens. The only way stock leaves is redeem, which burns the deed in the same transaction. An emptied note cannot exist, nobody can be sold a spent certificate.

3. Bearer note

Whoever holds the deed owns the vault. Trade it, gift it, or list it, the stock travels sealed inside. Royalty is 3.33% (cap 5%) to the treasury via setRoyalty.

4. Redeem and cash out

Redeem burns the deed and opens the vault into your wallet. From there, one tap sells the stock back to a machine at 95% of the live mark.

VRNG on Robinhood Chain, powered by DERP

Robinhood Chain has no Chainlink VRF on the production path, so the Box draws randomness from DERP: miners publish certified entropy prints to an onchain conductor, and every machine reads its random words from that shared feed. Production machines use 3 prints ahead plus a ~45s settle-delay floor (older builds used 8 prints / 60s). Round time is cadence-bound, typically a couple of minutes; the UI shows a live ETA from mine health. A Chainlink VRF conductor adapter exists for a future migration.

  • Commit first, entropy later. A pull commits to a word that does not exist yet, derived from future certified prints. Neither the player, the house, nor any single miner can choose the outcome. Once the deciding print lands, the ticket cannot be cancelled to dodge a bad roll.
  • Anyone can verify. wordOf(id) equals previewWord(id) once fulfilled. Anyone can recompute the multiplier; our keeper (or any third party) can call fulfill to land the word.
  • Fails closed. If certified prints thin out or stall, machines stop selling new tickets (FloorUnhealthy). Settles, rescues, redeems, and cash-outs always go through. A pull whose word never lands can be rescued for a full ticket refund after 48 hours.
  • Inventory-bound concurrency. Open rounds reserve stock; when free stock cannot back a new ticket the bell greys out until rounds settle or the restock keeper bridges ETH and converts bankroll to stock. Pulls never sell an uncovered prize.
